# Contributing

Anyone can contribute. That's the whole point.

## The basics

1. **Fork the repo.** Make your own copy.
2. **Make a change.** Fix a typo. Reword a confusing sentence. Propose a whole new amendment. Argue against an existing one.
3. **Open a pull request.** Explain what you changed and why. Use plain language.
4. **Discuss in the open.** Other contributors will weigh in. So will AI assistants that summarize the debate and flag issues.
5. **Merge or move on.** Changes that improve the document get merged. Changes that don't get a clear answer for why not.

## Rules

- **Plain English.** 8th-grade reading level or below. If a sentence needs a lawyer to parse, rewrite it.
- **Cite your sources.** If you claim something is true, link to evidence.
- **No personal attacks.** Argue with ideas, not people.
- **No corporate sock puppetry.** Disclose conflicts of interest. We will check.
- **Show your work.** Big changes need a rationale doc, not just a diff.

## Types of contributions we want

- **Clarity edits.** Make things easier to read.
- **Translations.** Spanish first. Then everything else.
- **Counter-proposals.** Disagree? Write a competing amendment. We'll read both.
- **Impact analysis.** Model the real-world effects of a proposal.
- **Historical context.** Has this been tried before? What happened?
- **Plain-language summaries.** TL;DR every long doc.

## What gets rejected

- Anything that re-introduces capture (corporate carve-outs, donor loopholes).
- Anything written to obscure rather than clarify.
- Anything that strips rights from people based on identity.

## Tooling

- **AI-assisted drafting is encouraged.** Use it. Disclose it.
- **Version everything.** Even your discussion notes.
- **Sign your commits** when you can. Reputation matters.

## Code of conduct

Treat other contributors the way you'd want to be treated in a town hall — heated debate is fine, contempt is not.
