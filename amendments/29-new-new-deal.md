## 3. Rationale

**Working thesis:** The original New Deal built the floor of the 20th century — Social Security, labor rights, public works. It's worn through. Housing costs, medical debt, student debt, and stagnant wages have made the middle-class contract described in high school civics textbooks unrecognizable to the people supposedly living it. Enumerating economic rights at the constitutional level is a response to a specific failure mode: the political system's habit of granting these rights during a crisis and dismantling them the moment the crisis passes.

## 8. Open questions

Expected pillars this amendment must address:

- **Housing.** A right to shelter. What form (voucher, public housing, guarantee)? What quality standard?
- **Healthcare.** A right to care. Single-payer, multi-payer with universal coverage, or something else?
- **Education.** A right to K–12 has partial state-level footing already; does this extend to pre-K and post-secondary?
- **A living wage.** Federal floor tied to a cost-of-living formula, not a fixed number Congress must revisit and never does.
- **Freedom from medical and educational debt bondage.** Caps, discharge conditions, or elimination.
- **The right to organize.** Constitutional backing for labor organizing, given how thoroughly the NLRA has been hollowed.
- **A guaranteed opportunity to work.** Federal job guarantee, or a weaker guarantee of retraining and transition support.
