# Amendment 30 — Direct Digital Democracy

**Model B — Sortition.** Congress is abolished. Direct democracy handles statute. A randomly selected citizen body — the People's Assembly — handles continuous legislative work that direct democracy cannot.

> This file is one of three competing drafts of Amendment 30. The other two — Pure Direct Democracy (Model A) and Hybrid (Model C) — live on separate branches. The repo will pick one, or merge the best of all three.

---

## Part 1 — The text

### Section 1. Establishment

The legislative power of the United States is vested jointly in the People of the United States, acting directly through a verified national vote, and in the People's Assembly, a body of citizens selected at random.

The Congress of the United States, including the Senate and the House of Representatives, is abolished. All legislative authority previously held by Congress passes to the People and the People's Assembly as set forth in this Article.

### Section 2. Eligibility

Every citizen of the United States who has attained the age of eighteen years is entitled to one vote on every federal legislative matter put to direct vote.

The right to vote shall not be denied or abridged on account of race, color, sex, religion, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, wealth, place of residence within the United States, criminal history, or political belief.

### Section 3. The People's Assembly

#### 3.1 Composition

The People's Assembly shall consist of four hundred (400) citizens, selected at random from the population of eligible voters by the Federal Election Authority, by means that are publicly auditable and demographically stratified by age, sex, region, and economic status to reflect the national population within reasonable tolerance.

#### 3.2 Service

Service in the Assembly is a civic duty, like jury service. A selected citizen may decline only for cause defined by enabling legislation. Selected citizens shall be paid not less than the median full-time wage in the United States, plus housing, child care, and travel, for the duration of their service.

#### 3.3 Term

The term of service is two years. Terms are staggered so that one half of the Assembly is replaced every year. No citizen may serve more than one term in any twenty-year period.

#### 3.4 Powers and duties

The People's Assembly shall:

1. Propose, debate, and pass federal statutes within its concurrent legislative authority under Section 5.
2. Approve the federal budget, with line-item authority.
3. Confirm executive and judicial nominees.
4. Ratify treaties by two-thirds vote.
5. Conduct oversight of the Executive branch, with subpoena power.
6. Declare war, subject to direct ratification under Section 5.

#### 3.5 Independence

Members of the Assembly are prohibited from receiving any compensation, gift, or thing of value above a de minimis threshold from any private source during their term and for ten years thereafter. Violation is a felony.

Members may not engage in employment with any federal contractor, registered foreign agent, or entity having business before the Assembly during their term and for ten years thereafter.

The Assembly shall be supported by a non-partisan professional staff and by public AI advisory systems whose code is open and whose recommendations are non-binding.

#### 3.6 Conduct

All Assembly proceedings shall be public. All votes shall be recorded by name. All communications between Assembly members and outside parties on matters of legislation shall be logged and published within seventy-two hours.

### Section 4. Proposals from the People

Any citizen may propose federal legislation by direct ballot. A proposal becomes a national ballot measure when it is supported by a number of citizens equal to one half of one percent (0.5%) of the previous national vote total.

Every citizen has the right to use AI drafting assistance, provided at public expense, to prepare proposed legislation.

### Section 5. Concurrent authority and direct ratification

#### 5.1 What the Assembly may pass alone

The Assembly may pass laws governing:

1. The federal budget and appropriations.
2. The structure and administration of executive departments.
3. The confirmation of nominees.
4. Treaty ratification.
5. Continuous regulatory authorization.

#### 5.2 What requires direct ratification

The following acts require ratification by direct vote of the People under Section 6:

1. Declarations of war and extensions of armed conflict beyond ninety days.
2. Federal tax changes raising or lowering revenue by more than one percent of gross domestic product.
3. Changes to criminal law.
4. Constitutional amendments proposed by the Assembly.
5. Any measure passed by the Assembly that ten percent of the previous national vote total signs a petition to put to direct vote within thirty days of passage.

#### 5.3 What the People may pass alone

The People may, by ballot measure under Section 6, pass any federal statute, override any Assembly act, or place a question before the Assembly for action.

### Section 6. Direct voting procedure

#### 6.1 Deliberation

A ballot measure shall be deliberated for not less than ninety days before a vote, during which time:

1. An impact analysis shall be prepared by not fewer than three independent public AI systems, with disclosed training data and source code, and published in full.
2. A plain-language summary, not exceeding one page, shall accompany the full text.
3. A disclosure of every person, organization, and expenditure supporting or opposing the measure shall be published and kept current.
4. Public deliberation forums shall be open to all citizens, in both digital and physical form, with paper participation available in every county.

#### 6.2 Voting

Voting on a ballot measure shall remain open for not less than fourteen consecutive days. A measure passes if:

1. A majority of votes cast favor it, and
2. The number of votes cast equals or exceeds twenty-five percent of eligible voters.

For Major measures as defined in Section 5.2, the threshold is sixty percent of votes cast and a quorum of forty percent of eligible voters.

Voting shall be conducted by means that are secure, auditable, accessible to citizens without internet access, and verifiable on paper.

### Section 7. Limits

Neither the People by direct vote nor the Assembly by its own act may:

1. Abridge any right protected by this Constitution.
2. Convict any person of a crime, or impose punishment on any named person.
3. Suspend the writ of habeas corpus, except as elsewhere provided in this Constitution.
4. Pass any bill of attainder or ex post facto law.
5. Override a final judgment of the Supreme Court on a constitutional question.

### Section 8. Emergency powers

In a declared national emergency, the President may, with the concurrence of the Vice President and the Chief Justice of the United States, issue temporary measures with the force of law for a period not exceeding thirty days. Such measures shall expire automatically and may be extended only by action of the Assembly, ratified by the People under Section 6.

### Section 9. Judicial review

All acts under this Article are subject to judicial review for conformity with this Constitution. Courts shall not strike down such acts on grounds of policy disagreement, but only for constitutional violation.

### Section 10. Sunset

Every act under this Article expires ten years from the date of passage, unless renewed by the same process by which it was originally passed.

### Section 11. Enabling legislation

The Federal Election Authority shall administer selection of the People's Assembly, manage direct voting, and operate the public AI drafting and analysis systems. It shall be funded independently of the executive branch and shall be auditable by any citizen.

## Part 2 — Plain-language commentary

**What this does in everyday terms:**

- **You vote on the big stuff. Directly.** War. Big tax changes. Criminal law. Anything you can get 0.5% of last election's voters to sign.
- **A jury of 400 random Americans handles the rest.** Like jury duty, but for legislation. Two years of service, paid a decent wage, supported by experts.
- **Congress is gone.** No Senate. No House. No campaigns. No fundraising. No safe seats.
- **You can be called to serve.** The Assembly is chosen at random, stratified so it actually looks like America. If your number comes up, you serve.
- **No more career politicians.** You serve two years, then you go home. You can't serve again for 20 years. You can't take a corporate job for 10 years after.
- **No money in politics.** Members can't take anything of value while serving or for 10 years after. Felony if they do.
- **The Assembly handles the boring, technical, continuous work.** Budgets, oversight, confirmations. The stuff direct democracy is genuinely bad at.
- **You can override the Assembly.** If they pass something you hate, 10% of last election's voters can force a direct vote on it.
- **Major things still require your vote.** The Assembly cannot declare war, raise big taxes, or change criminal law on their own.
- **All Assembly proceedings are public.** Every vote, every meeting, every conversation with outsiders logged within 3 days.

**What this does not do:**

- It does not require you to vote on every single thing. The Assembly handles continuous work. You vote on what matters most.
- It does not turn legislators into experts overnight. They have professional staff and open AI advisors. The expertise comes from the support system, not the lottery.
- It does not eliminate the Executive or the Courts. The President still administers. The Courts still adjudicate.

## Part 3 — Rationale

### Why sortition

Sortition — selection by lot — is one of the oldest democratic mechanisms in human history. Classical Athens used it to fill most public offices. Renaissance Italian city-states used it. Modern citizens' assemblies have been used in Ireland (abortion, marriage equality), France (climate), Iceland (constitutional reform), and dozens of smaller jurisdictions.

The pattern across these modern experiments is consistent: a randomly selected, demographically representative body, given time, expert support, and the authority to actually decide, produces decisions that are more deliberative, less partisan, and more trusted than elected bodies.

The mechanism is simple. **Elections select for the skills of getting elected.** Those skills — fundraising, telegenic charisma, partisan loyalty, message discipline — are not the skills of governing. Sortition selects from the whole population. The people who serve are not the people who wanted the job badly enough to spend a decade climbing a party ladder. They are the people who got the call.

### Why this model abolishes Congress entirely

The same arguments as Model A apply, with an addition specific to this model:

**1. Sortition only works if it has real power.** Modern citizen assemblies have been impressive but advisory. When the recommendations of a citizen assembly are non-binding, governments cherry-pick what they like and ignore the rest. To work, sortition has to *be* the legislature. Not a side panel. Not a focus group. The legislature.

**2. Concurrent authority creates accountability without paralysis.** The Assembly does sustained work. The People retain override and direct-action authority. Neither dominates.

**3. The expertise concern is solved by structure, not by selection.** The Assembly is supported by professional non-partisan staff and open AI advisory systems. Expertise enters through the support system, not through who serves. This is how juries handle complex civil and criminal cases now — they are not selected for expertise; they are supported by it.

### What this model is good at

- **It breaks the lobbying business model.** You cannot lobby someone who got the job by lottery, serves for two years, and is barred from corporate employment for a decade afterward. The current ROI on lobbying disappears.
- **It produces real demographic representation.** Stratified random selection means the Assembly looks like America — by age, race, region, income — in a way Congress has never managed.
- **It handles continuous, technical work.** Direct democracy is bad at budget detail and ongoing oversight. The Assembly is good at it.
- **It uses direct democracy where direct democracy is best.** Major moral and political questions. Things that require the legitimacy of the whole population, not a sample.
- **It has historical precedent.** This is not science fiction. It is the oldest democratic mechanism we have, recovered.

### What this model is bad at — and we are not hiding this

**Unfamiliarity.** Most Americans have never heard of sortition. The rationale has to do real educational work. A movement that depends on explaining what a "stratified random sample" is has a steeper road than one that says "you vote on everything."

**Selection legitimacy.** "I didn't choose them, they were chosen randomly" feels different from "I voted for them." The counter-argument — "you didn't really choose your representatives either, the donors did" — is true but requires explanation. The randomness has to be visibly, auditably fair.

**The 400 number is a guess.** Too small and it's not representative; too large and it can't deliberate. 400 is bigger than most modern citizen assemblies (which run 100–250) but small enough to function as one body. We expect this to be argued.

**Mob rule risk inside the Assembly.** A randomly selected body is still subject to bad incentives — groupthink, cascade effects, capture by professional staff. The mitigations are the same as for any deliberative body: public proceedings, recorded votes, logged communications, judicial review on constitutional questions. The mitigations are not perfect.

**Mob rule risk in direct vote.** Same as Model A. See Section 7 and the Bill of Rights firewall.

**Capture of the support apparatus.** If the staff and AI advisors that support the Assembly are captured, the Assembly is captured. Sections 3.5 and 3.6 — independence, conduct, open AI code — are necessary but not sufficient. The Federal Election Authority's independence from the executive matters enormously here.

**Continuity.** Two-year terms mean expertise turns over. Staggered terms (Section 3.3) help. A standing professional staff helps. Open AI institutional memory helps. We accept that there is some loss of continuity as a feature, not a bug — institutional knowledge that lasts forever becomes institutional capture.

### The serious case against this model

The strongest argument against sortition is that ordinary citizens, even with support, cannot reliably handle complex modern legislation. We disagree, and we point to the actual record of modern citizen assemblies — Ireland's abortion referendum and France's climate convention are the marquee examples. The decisions were thoughtful, deeply researched, and broadly supported. The participants started uninformed and finished informed. The system worked.

But we are honest: those were single-issue assemblies, with limited time, on contained questions. A standing Assembly handling everything from budget detail to military oversight is a bigger ask. The 400-member size, two-year terms, professional staff, and open AI support are our attempt to scale the model. We will not pretend the scaling problem is solved before it has been tried.

### The competing models

This is Model B. Two siblings exist:

- **Model A — Pure Direct Democracy.** Citizens vote on everything directly. No standing body.
- **Model C — Hybrid.** Keep a small elected technical Senate for sustained work. Abolish the House. Direct democracy replaces the House's general legislative function.

Read all three. Argue. Vote.
