## 3. Rationale

**Working thesis:** Representative democracy was designed for a country where information traveled at the speed of a horse and a citizen could not credibly participate in a national debate. Neither constraint holds. The current system — professional politicians filtering the will of the public through lobbyists and party machines — is not a fixed feature of self-government. It's a workaround for constraints that no longer exist. This amendment asks: what does self-government look like when citizens can actually participate?

Full rationale to be developed alongside the competing drafts.

## 7. Objections and responses

**TODO.** Anticipated objections:
- **Tyranny of the majority.** Direct democracy without protections repeatedly produces rights violations.
- **Voter capacity.** Legislation is complex; expecting every citizen to become an expert is unrealistic.
- **Turnout skew and fatigue.** Frequent votes produce lower and less representative turnout.
- **Identity and sybil resistance.** All of the problems in `process/00-how-this-works.md §5`, at national scale.
- **The role of AI.** Citizen-authored + AI-assisted legislation creates new capture vectors (whoever controls the AI).

## 9. Alternatives considered

Three approaches currently drafted on parallel branches:

- **`amendment-30/pure-dd`** — full direct democracy for federal legislation, Congress reduced to executing citizen-passed laws. Highest legitimacy, highest exposure to majoritarian and capture risks.
- **`amendment-30/hybrid`** — citizen-initiated legislation must pass both a public vote *and* a deliberative body. Congress reformed, not eliminated.
- **`amendment-30/sortition`** — a randomly selected citizen assembly (rotating, term-limited, well-compensated) replaces or complements the House. Draws on Athenian precedent and modern citizens' assemblies.

Each branch will produce a full amendment draft. The final v0 of Amendment 30 will pick one — or synthesize — based on public discussion.
