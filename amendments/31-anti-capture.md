## 3. Rationale

**Working thesis:** The most consistent finding in political science over the past several decades is that policy outcomes in the US track the preferences of the affluent and organized interest groups, not median voters. (See Gilens & Page 2014 and follow-up work.) Every other amendment in this slate fails if legislators still answer to donors and lobbyists first. Anti-capture is the load-bearing structural fix.

## 8. Open questions

Expected components:

- **Ban on paid lobbying.** Full ban, or a re-defined and heavily regulated version?
- **100% public campaign finance.** Voucher-based (each citizen gets a fixed amount to allocate), matching funds, block grants, or hybrid?
- **Revolving-door restrictions.** Lifetime ban between government service and lobbying? Between government and specific regulated industries? Cooling-off periods?
- **Dark money.** Total disclosure requirements. Definition of "electioneering communication" that survives free-speech scrutiny.
- **Corporate personhood.** Does this amendment address it directly, or leave it to *Citizens United*'s displacement to handle?
- **Enforcement body.** The FEC is famously deadlocked by design; what replaces it?
