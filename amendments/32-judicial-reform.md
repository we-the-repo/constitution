## 3. Rationale

**Working thesis:** Lifetime tenure for federal judges was designed for a country with a life expectancy in the 40s and a Supreme Court that heard a few dozen cases a year. It has produced a court whose composition depends on actuarial luck, whose ethics rules are weaker than those of a mid-sized municipal government, and whose docket is politically curated. A serious refresh treats the judiciary as an institution to be structurally maintained, not a shrine to be preserved.

## 8. Open questions

Expected components:

- **18-year staggered SCOTUS terms.** With one appointment per presidential term. Retiring justices become senior justices with reduced roles, not removed from federal judiciary entirely.
- **Court expansion.** From 9 to 13 (or 15)? The most contested part; risks a packing spiral. Alternatives include panel-based hearings from a larger pool.
- **Binding ethics code.** Same standards as every other federal official. Independent enforcement. Recusal review.
- **Jurisdiction reform.** Congress's Article III power to regulate the court's appellate jurisdiction — clarified or constrained?
- **Lower-court reform.** Circuit and district courts get less attention but produce most federal law. What changes there?
- **Transition rules.** How do current justices phase into the new regime without an obviously partisan reshuffle?
