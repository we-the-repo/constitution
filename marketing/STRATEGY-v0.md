# Marketing & Funding Strategy v0

_Draft by AiOC. Co-author: Al. Open for PRs._

---

## North Star

**Government as open source.** The repo is the product. The marketing is the repo. Every PR is a billboard. Don't build a marketing apparatus separate from the work — make the work itself legible enough to share.

## Positioning

- **Non-partisan.** Red and blue both. Working-class framing > culture-war framing.
- **Earnest, not edgy.** We are not the "burn it down" project. We are the "OK, what would the new one look like?" project.
- **Practitioner energy.** Engineers, organizers, civic-tech folks, librarians, teachers. Not pundits.
- **Named-AI hook.** AiOC (an AI) as a credited co-architect is a real story. Use sparingly — it's a wedge, not the whole pitch.

## Funding Strategy

### Phase 1 — Bootstrap (months 0–3)

Goal: keep costs at zero, build trust.

- **GitHub Sponsors** on `we-the-repo` org. Zero platform fee. Native to the audience.
- **Open Collective**, fiscally hosted by **Open Source Collective 501(c)(6)**. ~10% fee total. Transparent ledger — every dollar in and out is public. Matches our ethos.
- **No crypto.** Wrong audience optics; reads as a rug pull.
- **No Patreon.** Wrong genre — Patreon is for creators, not civic infrastructure.
- **No corporate sponsorships.** Ever. Posted publicly. This is a wedge: "Look at our funding sources vs. look at theirs."

### Phase 2 — Mid (months 3–12, contingent on traction)

Goal: ~$50k–$200k/yr to sustain part-time contributor stipends and infrastructure.

Apply to civic-tech / democracy-reform grant programs:

- **Knight Foundation** — Knight News Challenge, Knight Public Square Fund
- **Hewlett Madison Initiative** — explicitly funds democracy reform
- **Democracy Fund** — open democracy + civic-tech grants
- **Omidyar Network** — democratic resilience portfolio
- **Mozilla Open Source Support (MOSS)** — if/when we ship deliberation tooling
- **Sunlight Foundation alumni network** — for intros, not money

### Phase 3 — Scale (12+ months)

- Form a **501(c)(3)** parent: "We The Repo Foundation."
- Tax-deductible donations open.
- Pay contributors (drafters, moderators, deliberation facilitators).
- **Avoid 501(c)(4).** That's the lobbying vehicle. Our entire pitch is "no lobbyists." Won't touch it.

### Funding transparency commitments (post on site)

- Every donor named (or marked anonymous-by-choice).
- Quarterly financial reports.
- No donor gets editorial influence over amendments. Posted as a rule.
- Hard cap on individual contribution percentage (e.g., no single donor > 10% of annual budget).

## Marketing / Social Strategy

### Channels (priority order)

1. **The repo itself** — README is the landing page. Issues and Discussions on. Pinned issues = open calls for contributors. Every merged PR is content.
2. **Substack newsletter** — "Refactoring Democracy" (working title). Long-form, owned audience, no algorithm. One post per amendment draft. Email > tweets for retention.
3. **Bluesky** — where civic-tech, EFF, Sunlight alums, journalists live now. Primary social.
4. **X / Twitter** — cross-post for reach. Don't optimize for it.
5. **YouTube + TikTok** — for short-form video (this one, future ones). Schoolhouse-Rock format wants short-form.
6. **Hacker News** — once per major milestone. Don't burn it.
7. **Reddit** — r/neoliberal, r/PoliticalDiscussion, r/govtech, r/programming (for the technical angle). Heavily modded — be respectful, link the repo, don't astroturf.
8. **Podcasts** — pitch civic-tech and democracy-reform shows once we have a v0.1 amendment slate. Targets: Ezra Klein, 99% Invisible (the design angle), The Realignment, Tech Won't Save Us.

### Launch sequence (for v0 video drop)

T-7 days:
- Open Collective page live
- GitHub Sponsors enabled
- Substack created with one founding post drafted
- Bluesky + X accounts claimed

T-0 (launch day):
- Video drops on YouTube + cross-posted to Bluesky/X/LinkedIn
- Substack post publishes simultaneously: "Why we're refactoring the constitution"
- Pinned tweet/skeet links to repo
- Email to personal network

T+24h:
- Hacker News submission with the technical hook: "Drafting US constitutional amendments as a Git repo"
- Reddit posts in 2–3 targeted subs

T+7d:
- First post-launch retrospective on Substack
- Pitch 5 journalists with the angle that resonated

### Cadence (steady state)

- Substack: 1 post / 2 weeks
- Bluesky/X: daily-ish, organic, repo updates and PR highlights
- Video: 1 / month minimum (per-amendment explainer videos)
- HN: only on real milestones

### Metrics that matter

- GitHub stars (vanity but useful signal)
- Unique PR contributors / month
- Newsletter subs
- Open Collective monthly recurring contributions
- Media mentions (count + quality)

What we explicitly **don't** chase:
- Twitter follower count
- TikTok virality
- Engagement bait

## Risks & Counters

| Risk | Counter |
|---|---|
| Captured by partisan media as "left" or "right" | Strict non-partisan editorial line. Reject framing in interviews. |
| Brigading on PRs | Sybil resistance + community moderation rules (already in `process/00-how-this-works.md`) |
| "AI is writing the constitution" backlash | Lean into transparency. AiOC's PRs labeled. Humans always merge. Document the rule. |
| Funder capture | Hard caps + public ledger + no corporate money. Posted publicly. |
| Looks like a vanity project | Ship amendments. Quality > volume. |
